Core principles

Five things I hold to

Hover a principle to read the full story ↓ — / 05
Inclusion · 01/05

Thin-file applicants deserve a fair read

People with little credit history are the easiest to wrongly decline. Better features — banking behaviour, bureau signals — give them a fairer read than a blunt cutoff ever could.

Alternative signals → Fairer access
Speed · 02/05

Speed is access too

Automating real-time scoring inside the underwriting flow cut turnaround by 30%. A decision in seconds instead of days is a materially different experience for someone who needs the money now.

Instant decisioning −30% turnaround
Proactive · 03/05

Catching trouble early

The EMI bounce model finds who is about to miss a payment, so they get a call before it becomes a default — 40% of at-risk customers recovered.

86% AUC · 77% capture +40% recovery
Governance · 04/05

Explainability isn't optional

If a model declines someone, there has to be a reason you can say out loud — to a regulator, a customer, or yourself. That's why I reach for scorecards before black boxes.

Interpretable by design → Signed off
GenAI · 05/05

Where LLMs actually help

Not in making the decision — in everything around it. I built a RAG chatbot that answers from internal documents with the source attached. Retrieval with a citation is useful; a language model guessing a credit decision is not.

Document-grounded → Cited answers

"A model that can't be explained doesn't get deployed — and shouldn't."
